Plenty of freelance copywriters can write a clean, persuasive sentence. Far fewer can write a page that actually shows up when someone searches for it. The gap between "good writing" and "SEO copywriting" usually comes down to a handful of repeatable mistakes, and once you know what to look for, they're easy to spot in almost any freelancer's portfolio, including work I wrote early on myself.

## Mistake #1: Keyword Stuffing Instead of Keyword Context

The oldest habit in SEO writing is also the easiest to overdo: repeating the target keyword until the copy sounds like it was written for a search engine instead of a person. A page that says "plumber in Austin" six times in four paragraphs doesn't rank better for it. Modern search algorithms are built to recognize and often penalize exactly that pattern.

**The fix:** use the primary keyword naturally where it belongs: the title, one H2, the opening paragraph, and a closing mention. Then fill the rest of the page with related terms and phrases a real customer would use. Write the sentence first, and only check whether the keyword fits afterward. If it doesn't fit naturally, don't force it.

## Mistake #2: Ignoring Search Intent Entirely

A page can be well-written, keyword-optimized, and still fail if it answers the wrong question. Someone searching "how much does a website redesign cost" wants a ballpark figure and pricing factors, not a sales pitch about your design philosophy. Someone searching "best website redesign agency" is already comparison-shopping and wants proof, not education.

Freelancers who skip intent research write one generic version of a topic and expect it to satisfy every kind of searcher. It won't. Google's own ranking signals increasingly reward pages that match the specific intent behind a query (informational, comparison, or transactional) over pages that are simply "about" the topic.

**The fix:** before writing a word, search the target keyword yourself and look at what's already ranking. If the results are all list-style comparisons, a single-product sales page won't outrank them no matter how well it's written. Match the format search is already telling you it wants.

## Mistake #3: Weak or Missing Meta Descriptions

A meta description doesn't directly move rankings, but it's the single biggest lever most freelancers leave untouched. It's the pitch that decides whether someone clicks your result or the one above or below it. I still see meta descriptions left as the default first sentence of the page, or worse, left blank entirely and auto-generated by the CMS.

**The fix:** write a meta description as its own piece of copy, not an afterthought. Lead with the specific benefit or answer, include the primary keyword naturally, and stay under roughly 155 characters so it doesn't get cut off in search results. Treat it like ad copy, because functionally, that's exactly what it is.

## Mistake #4: No Internal Linking Strategy

A page that exists in isolation, with no links pointing to or from other relevant pages on the site, is harder for search engines to understand and rank, and harder for visitors to explore. Freelancers who deliver copy page by page, without thinking about how those pages connect, leave real ranking potential on the table.

**The fix:** every time you write a page, ask what other pages on the site it should link to, and which existing pages should link back to it. A blog post about "signs you need a website redesign" should link to the redesign service page, and that service page should link back to the post. This isn't a technical SEO task bolted on afterward. It's part of the writing process.

## Mistake #5: Thin Content That Doesn't Actually Answer the Question

Hitting an arbitrary word count isn't the goal, but a page that's genuinely too thin to answer the visitor's question won't compete against pages that go deeper. I regularly see freelance-written service pages at 200-300 words competing for keywords where the top-ranking pages run 1,000+ words and address pricing, process, objections, and FAQs in detail.

**The fix:** before writing, list every question a real visitor would have about the topic: pricing, timeline, process, what makes this option different. Then make sure the page answers each one. Depth isn't padding a page with filler; it's making sure nothing a visitor needs is missing.

## The Pattern Behind All Five

Every one of these mistakes comes from writing for the page instead of writing for the search and the searcher. Keyword stuffing writes for an algorithm that no longer rewards it. Ignoring intent writes for the writer's assumption of what the topic means. Thin content writes to finish the page instead of finish answering the question. Fixing all five is less about learning new tricks and more about reversing that default: start from what the searcher actually needs, and let the SEO fundamentals follow from there.
