## Challenge

A local directory client needed to scale from a handful of hand-written location and service pages to 50+ pages covering every city and category combination they served. Their in-house process meant briefing a writer, waiting days for a draft, formatting it in WordPress, and manually checking on-page SEO basics before it ever went live. At that pace, covering their full service area would have taken months and tied up a writer full-time.

They wanted the coverage without hiring another writer, and without sacrificing quality control — every page still needed a human sign-off before it touched the live site.

## Approach

I built an n8n workflow that handles the entire pipeline from a single input (city + service) to a publish-ready draft:

- A trigger step pulls target keyword and location data from a Google Sheet queue.
- An AI research node gathers local context (landmarks, service specifics, competitor angles) and passes it into a structured drafting prompt.
- A formatting step converts the draft into clean HTML with proper heading hierarchy, internal links, and schema-ready metadata.
- The finished draft is pushed to WordPress as a **draft post** via the REST API, tagged for review rather than published live.
- A Slack notification alerts the QA reviewer with a direct edit link.
- Once approved, a one-click action in n8n flips the post status to "publish" through the same API connection — no manual WordPress editing required.

The workflow was built and tested over three weeks, with the first 10 pages used to tune the prompt structure before scaling to the full batch.

## Result

The client went from zero to 50+ published pages in under six weeks, with every page still passing through a human QA gate before going live. Manual writer hours per page dropped to zero — the only human touchpoint became a five-minute approve/reject decision. Compared to their prior manual workflow, the end-to-end time per page dropped by roughly 70%, and the client has since reused the same pipeline to keep expanding into new service areas without adding headcount.
