## Challenge

A multi-practice law firm had service pages for six practice areas that never ranked beyond page two or three for terms like "family law attorney near me" or "criminal defense lawyer near me." The pages themselves were the core problem. Each one ran only 300 to 400 words of dense legal language written the way attorneys talk to each other, not the way a stressed, searching client reads.

There were no FAQ sections to catch the long-tail questions people actually type into Google. There was no local SEO signal woven into the copy, just a city name mentioned once near the top and nothing else tying the page to the region it served. And every page ended the same way, with a flat, generic "Contact Us" button that gave a visitor no reason to act right now instead of closing the tab and searching for the next firm.

The firm had good attorneys and a strong track record, but none of that was visible to Google or to the person landing on the page. A few specific issues kept showing up across every practice area:

- **Thin content couldn't compete.** At 300 to 400 words, the pages had nowhere near the depth needed to rank for competitive local legal terms.
- **No FAQ structure meant no long-tail visibility.** A huge share of legal searches are questions, and the site had no pages built to answer them directly.
- **Local SEO was missing almost entirely.** No consistent city or region integration, no local business or legal service schema markup.
- **The calls to action asked for too much, too soon.** "Contact Us" is a big ask for someone who is anxious and just started researching; it gave them no easy first step.

## Approach

Each of the six practice area pages was rebuilt from scratch, not edited, using the same underlying framework applied consistently across all six.

**Client-First Structure**
Every page now opens by naming the visitor's exact situation before saying anything about the firm. Someone searching for a divorce attorney reads their own circumstances described back to them in the first two sentences, not a paragraph about the firm's history and awards.

**Depth Through Real Expertise**
Pages were expanded from 300 to 400 words up to 1,200 to 1,800 words, but the added length is not filler. It comes directly from the attorneys' actual answers to the questions their real clients ask during consultations, turned into structured, readable copy.

**FAQ Sections With Schema Markup**
Each page now includes a dedicated FAQ section targeting the long-tail, question-based searches that make up a large share of legal search volume, marked up with FAQ schema so Google can surface these answers directly in search results.

**Local SEO Integration**
City and region names are woven naturally throughout the copy instead of appearing once near the top, and Attorney and LegalService schema markup was added to every page so search engines can clearly understand who the firm is, where it operates, and what it practices.

**Specific, Low-Friction Calls to Action**
Generic "Contact Us" buttons were replaced with specific, low-commitment actions like "Schedule a Free 15-Minute Consultation," giving anxious visitors a smaller, easier first step instead of an open-ended ask.

## Results

Across all six rebuilt practice area pages, rankings, traffic, and lead volume moved together and moved fast.

| Metric | Before | After |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Ranking position (target keywords) | Page 2 to 3 | **#1 to 3** |
| Organic traffic to practice pages | 480/mo | **2,100/mo** |
| Consultation requests from organic | ~3/mo | **~22/mo** |
| Average time on page | 51s | **2m 04s** |

The ranking movement followed a clear curve. Target keywords sat around position 45 on average before the rewrite, dropped to position 22 within the first month, reached position 9 by month two, and landed at position 3 or better by month three across the target terms.

> "Prospective clients are anxious when they're searching for a lawyer. These pages finally speak to that, and Google noticed too. We're now on the first page for the searches that actually bring in cases."
>
> Managing Partner, Multi-Practice Law Firm

The jump in average time on page, from under a minute to over two minutes, mattered just as much as the ranking gains. It signaled that people weren't just landing on the pages, they were actually reading them, which is exactly what a longer, more useful page is supposed to do.

## Key Takeaways

- **Write for the client's situation, not the firm's resume.** Leading with credentials and history pushes the reader's actual problem further down the page, right where they're most likely to leave.
- **FAQ sections are a double win.** They capture long-tail search traffic and handle common objections and questions in the same section, doing two jobs with one piece of content.
- **Depth signals authority to both readers and Google.** Thin pages compete on neither front; a well-structured 1,200 to 1,800 word page gives both the algorithm and the human reader enough substance to trust.
- **Small, specific CTAs outperform generic ones.** Asking for a free 15-minute consultation is a far smaller commitment than "Contact Us," and smaller commitments convert better with anxious, early-stage searchers.
