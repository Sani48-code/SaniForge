## Challenge

A multi-location law firm covering personal injury, family law, criminal defense, and estate planning had thin, outdated service pages that read more like generic boilerplate than pages built to rank or convert. None of their four core practice areas showed up on the first page of Google for the terms that mattered most, and prospective clients landing on the site had no clear sense of why they should call this firm over the next listing.

The firm needed pages that could compete for competitive local legal keywords while still meeting the trust and credibility bar that legal content requires.

## Approach

I ran keyword research for each practice area to identify the highest-intent local search terms, then mapped search intent against what competing firms were (and weren't) covering. Each service page was rebuilt from scratch around:

- A clear E-E-A-T structure: attorney credentials, case result context, and firm experience surfaced near the top of the page rather than buried below generic copy.
- Location-specific language for each of the firm's service areas, avoiding duplicate content across pages.
- On-page SEO fundamentals — optimized title tags, header hierarchy, internal linking between related practice areas, and FAQ sections targeting long-tail questions.
- LegalService and Attorney schema markup added to each page to support rich results and improve how search engines parsed the firm's credentials.

Pages were written and shipped over a four-week span, one practice area at a time, so each could be reviewed by the firm's attorneys for accuracy before publishing.

## Result

Within about three months of publishing, all four rewritten practice area pages reached the first page of Google for their primary target keywords, with three landing in the top 3 positions. Organic traffic to the practice area section of the site became the firm's top source of new client inquiries, and the schema-backed pages began surfacing in rich result panels for branded and practice-area searches alike.
